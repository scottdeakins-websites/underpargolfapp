Ask most golfers how far they hit their 7-iron and you'll get their best one ever. That's why so many approach shots come up short. A **yardage guide** is a written record of what each club *actually* does. It takes the guessing out of club selection, and it's one of the simplest ways to save strokes without changing your swing.

## What to record for each club

| Column | What it means |
|---|---|
| Loft | Check the real number. Modern lofts are often stronger than the label suggests, so look for even gaps, especially in your wedges. |
| Punch | Distance with a deliberate knockdown or choked-down swing (about 50–75%) |
| Stock | Your average full-swing distance |
| Max | Your longest solid shot, not counting thin or bladed ones |
| Carry / Total | For clubs that roll out, write both, e.g. **150/155** |

## How to find your numbers

1. **Use a launch monitor or simulator if you can.** You'll get carry and total for every shot.
2. **Hit 5–7 full shots with each club.**
3. **Throw out the clear mishits:** chunks, thins, shanks.
4. **Average the rest.** That's your stock yardage. Your longest remaining shot is your max.
5. **Repeat with punch shots** and record the average.

No simulator? Use the range and a rangefinder, or collect numbers on the course over a few rounds. Measure on calm days, because wind ruins the data. Update the guide whenever your distances change: new clubs, a swing change, or a winter in the gym.

## Using it on the course

For every approach, know three numbers:

1. **Front ("cover") distance:** what you need to carry to clear the bunker or rough short of the green.
2. **Pin distance:** to the flag.
3. **Back distance:** to the back edge. This matters most when the pin is near the back and long means a hard up-and-down.

Your max number is especially useful here. If the pin is 160 and there's trouble just past it, a club whose max is 165 is too much. Take less, and accept a longer putt.

## Fill in the wedge gaps

Most golfers have big holes between their wedges. Choking down 1 and 2 inches fills them with shots you've actually measured.
