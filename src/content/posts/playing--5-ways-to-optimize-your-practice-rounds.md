*By Michael Leonard*

After nearly 200 tournament days, I've learned that a practice round has one job: **build your game plan.** Your score doesn't matter. With nothing on the line it's easy to drift, so go in with a purpose.

## 1. Use a yardage book

Get one from the pro shop or online, ideally with a detailed slope map of each green. Write in carry distances, your club choices, wind direction and slope. A cover keeps it in good shape for years.

## 2. Build your tee strategy

- Decide the club for every tee. Driver suits most par 4s and 5s, but tight holes with trouble may call for a 3-wood or hybrid.
- Try a few different tee shots to find the best option.
- Pick the [best side of the tee box](/blogs/playing-strategies-for-golfers/3-quick-tips-to-optimizing-angles-off-the-tee) for your shot shape.
- Note carries over hazards and fairway bunkers, and the wind.
- Measure slope on approaches and write it down. You usually can't use a rangefinder's slope feature in competition.

## 3. Study the greens

How does each green take different shots? Do wedges check up or release? How far does a 6-iron roll out? Hit full and [flighted wedges](/blogs/full-swing-guide-from-scratch-golf/the-art-of-hitting-flighted-wedges) to compare.

## 4. Test the short game

From fringe, fairway, rough and sand: how does the ball react? Hit chips and pitches from each lie, and a few bunker shots to learn the sand's depth and consistency.

## 5. Putt a lot

Uphill, downhill, sidehill, long and short. Don't just putt to the flag: mark likely tournament pin spots with a tee and putt to those.

## Wrap-up

Walk off with a plan for every hole, then stick to it in the event so emotions don't make your decisions. Can't play the course first?
