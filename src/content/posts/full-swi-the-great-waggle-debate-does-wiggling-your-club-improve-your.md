Watch any tour event and you'll see it: a little back-and-forth of the club before the swing. Sergio Garcia was famous for over-waggling, sometimes as many as 25 times. Tony Finau uses a waggle to get his short, powerful swing started. So does it actually help?

## What it is

<div class="yt"><iframe src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/MR4ZX3cPUpQ" title="The golf waggle" loading="lazy" allow="accelerometer; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture" allowfullscreen></iframe></div>


A small, controlled back-and-forth movement of the club before the takeaway. Some golfers do a few, some do lots, some don't do any.

## The case for it

- **Feel:** a sense of the club's weight and balance before you swing.
- **Rhythm:** it sets a tempo for the swing that follows.
- **Tension release:** it loosens the hands and wrists.
- **Confidence:** for many players, it's a trigger that says "go."

There's limited research on it, and the benefits look mostly psychological: it doesn't change the swing so much as get you ready to make it.

## The case against it

- **Too many waggles** disrupt timing and tempo.
- **It can feed indecision:** another chance to start second-guessing.

## The verdict

It depends on you. Test it in practice: hit 10 balls with one consistent waggle and 10 without. If it helps, make it the same every time as part of your [pre-shot routine](/blogs/mindset/a-simple-blueprint-for-your-pre-shot-routine), and keep the whole routine quick.
