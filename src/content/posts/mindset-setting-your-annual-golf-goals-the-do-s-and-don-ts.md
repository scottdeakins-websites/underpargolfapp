Hoping for a good season isn't a plan. The golfers who improve most set clear goals, build a plan to reach them and adjust as the year goes on. The change of season is a natural time to do it, but any time works.

## Do: pick result goals you can measure

"Win the club championship" puts all the pressure on one week. Better result goals track stats you can measure and improve all year:

| Result goal | Example target |
|---|---|
| Short putts | Make 80% from inside 3 feet |
| Short game | Get up and down 40% of the time from inside 20 yards |
| Tee shot | Hit 60% of fairways |

Set the numbers from where you are now, not where a tour player is.

## Do: add process goals

The most common mistake is setting *only* result goals. Process goals are the actions you control that produce results:

- Practice short game **2 hours a week** for the season.
- Use the same [pre-shot routine](/blogs/mindset/a-simple-blueprint-for-your-pre-shot-routine) on every shot.

Pair every result goal with at least one process goal.

## Don't: make them too easy

Goals should get you out of bed to practice. Finding the line between unrealistic and too easy is hard. When in doubt, aim a little high.

## Do: review them regularly

Don't write them in January and forget them. Keep them in your phone, check them every couple of weeks, and make sure your practice plan still supports them. Raise the bar if you're ahead; refocus if a new weakness appears.
