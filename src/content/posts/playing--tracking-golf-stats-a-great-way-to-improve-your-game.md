You can't improve what you don't measure. Tracking stats gives you a baseline, tells you exactly where you're losing strokes and makes every practice session more efficient.

## Which stats to track

**Starting out:**

| Stat | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Fairways hit | Tee shot accuracy |
| Greens in regulation (GIR) | Approach play |
| Scrambling | Saving par after missing the green |
| Sand saves | Bunker play |
| Total putts | Putting |

**More advanced:** move to **strokes gained**, which compares every shot to a benchmark and tells you more than raw counts do. Mark Broadie's *Every Shot Counts* is the best introduction.

## How to track

- **Use an app, not a notebook.** Arccos, Golfshot and 18Birdies are simple and give you a round summary. Data-heavy players should look at **DECADE**, Scott Fawcett's course management system built on strokes-gained data.
- **Log every round the same night.** Make it a habit.
- **Get a baseline** of 5–10 rounds before you change your practice.

## Tour benchmarks

For context, here were the 2021 PGA Tour averages:

| Stat | Tour average |
|---|---|
| Driving accuracy | about 61% |
| Greens in regulation | about 65% |
| Scrambling | about 58% |
| Sand saves | about 50% |
| Putts per round | about 29 |

Those look low, and for a scratch player they're strong targets. But **compare yourself to yourself**, not to the tour.

## Turn stats into practice

Find your weakest number and give it the most practice time. That's step 1 of [how to build a golf practice plan](/blogs/the-blog/golf-practice-plan). Under Par Full Access coaches read their players' stats every week and adjust the plan from them.
