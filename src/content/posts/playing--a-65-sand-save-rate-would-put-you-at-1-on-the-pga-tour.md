Watch golf on TV and it looks like the pros get up and down from every bunker. They don't. TV shows the spectacular saves and skips most of the ones that end with a 12-footer missed.

## The real numbers

In the 2021 season, **Brooks Koepka led the PGA Tour with a sand save rate of about 65%.** A rate around **50%** was enough to rank among the top 100. Those players are hitting from immaculately maintained bunkers. Yours probably aren't.

## What that means for you

- **Missing a sand save is normal**, even for the best players in the world.
- **Expecting to save every bunker shot** adds pressure that makes the next one harder.
- **Set a realistic benchmark.** Track your own rate for a few rounds and try to improve on *that*, not on Koepka's.

## How to practice bunker play

- In the [save-par short game session](/blogs/short-game-improvement-guide/short-game-practice-session-to-help-you-save-par), hit two balls each from 10, 15, 20 and 25 yards and score how many finish inside 15 feet.
- Add awkward lies: footprints, plugged balls and downslopes. Here's why to [practice from bad lies](/blogs/the-blog/practice-tip-practice-from-bad-lies).
- Then putt out every ball. That's a real sand save.

Stay confident in the bunker, and go easy on yourself when one doesn't work out.
