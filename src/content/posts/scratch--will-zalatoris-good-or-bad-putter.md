Will Zalatoris has one of the most criticized putting strokes in golf: short, a little jerky, and a regular target online. But how a stroke looks and how well it works aren't the same thing.

## What the numbers said (as of mid-2022)

<img src="/assets/img/US_Open_-_Strokes_Gained_Putting.webp" alt="2022 U.S. Open strokes gained putting for Zalatoris and McIlroy" loading="lazy">


- In the 2021 PGA Tour season, his strokes gained putting was essentially **tour average**.
- In major championships since 2020, though, his strokes gained putting was among the **very best in golf** heading into the 2022 U.S. Open.
- His major finishes backed it up: **7th** at the 2021 Masters, **a playoff loss at the 2022 PGA Championship** after holing an 8-footer on 18 to get there, and **4th** at the 2022 U.S. Open.

## Why the gap?

- **Fast, sloping major greens** may suit him better than the average tour setup.
- **Short memory.** His coach, Scott Fawcett, has said Zalatoris moves on from misses quickly instead of dwelling on them.
- **Confidence from his iron play** may carry over to the greens in big events.

## What it means for your putting

1. **Judge your stroke by results, not looks.** If it goes in, it works.
2. **Context matters.** Your stats on fast greens might look very different from slow ones.
3. **Resilience is a putting skill.** A [post-shot routine](/blogs/mindset/the-post-shot-routine-learning-from-happy-gilmore-and-shooter-mcgavin) helps you let a miss go.
