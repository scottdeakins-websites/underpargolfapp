Everyone has rounds where the ball won't go where they're looking. What separates good players from average ones is what they shoot on those days. Five ways to keep the score respectable:

## 1. Play one shot shape

Pick the shape that's working, fade or draw, and use it all day. Trying to curve it both ways is hard even for tour players when they're off. Save the swing fixes for the range afterward.

## 2. Choke up

Go down about an inch on every club, driver and woods included. A shorter club is easier to control and more likely to find short grass. Hit lower, [knockdown-style](/blogs/full-swing-guide-from-scratch-golf/the-art-of-hitting-flighted-wedges) shots until the confidence comes back. Off the tee, this is the [fairway finder](/blogs/full-swing-guide-from-scratch-golf/3-steps-to-building-a-fairway-finder-off-the-tee).

## 3. One shot at a time

Don't add up the disaster in your head. Each bad shot is its own event, and it only affects the next one if you let it. Use your [post-shot routine](/blogs/mindset/the-post-shot-routine-learning-from-happy-gilmore-and-shooter-mcgavin) and let it go.

## 4. Play the percentages

Angry, aggressive golf turns bogeys into doubles.

- Off the tee, use the club and target you trust, even if that's a 3-wood or hybrid.
- Don't attack tucked pins from more than about 150 yards. Missing on the wrong side short-sides you.
- Stay conservative until your swing gives you a reason not to.

## 5. Fight to the last putt

Tiger is famous for grinding out rounds when he's far back or near the cut line. Keep your body language strong (eyes up, shoulders relaxed, chest up) and keep competing. The last few holes can still rescue a round.

If the bad days start stringing together, read [how to break out of a golf slump](/blogs/mindset/7-ways-to-break-out-of-a-golf-slump).
