*By Michael Leonard*

Course management isn't just hitting less than driver and aiming at the middle of the green. Done well, it's a decision system built on data. I've been using ideas from **DECADE**, Scott Fawcett's strokes-gained-based system that some tour players have used, and I've played some of my best golf since. Five takeaways:

## 1. Stop trying to shape every shot

Most golfers think they need to fade it into a right pin and draw it around a left dogleg. Even many elite players can't reliably hit more than one shape. **Play your natural shape 90% of the time.**

I'm a natural fader, so I aim left or left-center of the fairway even on left doglegs. With irons, my misses tend to finish on the right side of the green, and my confidence in my long game has gone way up.

## 2. Get out of trouble conservatively

After a wayward tee shot, the gap through the trees is tempting. The data says the hero shot is usually the wrong one; that urge is mostly ego. Get back into position. **A bogey is fine. A double isn't.**

## 3. Fix your body language

Frustrated body language feeds the next bad shot. Use the mantra **"eyes on the horizon":** head up, good posture, no matter what just happened.

## 4. Pick targets by distance

| Club in hand | Target |
|---|---|
| Wedge or short iron | Attack almost any flag |
| Mid iron | Flag only with plenty of room around it |
| Long iron, hybrid, wood | The fat part of the green; take the long birdie putt |

The longer the putt you leave, the more likely a three-putt, so aim closer with short clubs and safer with long ones.

## 5. Make more pars

The best rounds come from avoiding bogeys, not piling up birdies. Pars keep momentum; back-to-back bogeys kill it.
