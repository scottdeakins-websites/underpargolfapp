Great scorers aren't always great ball strikers. They're great planners. Five keys:

## 1. Calibrate your distances

Most amateurs take too little club because they use their *best* shot as their yardage, not their average. Build an honest [yardage guide](/blogs/full-swing-guide-from-scratch-golf/building-a-yardage-guide) and carry it on the course.

## 2. Play your shot shape

Most players have a dominant ball flight. Good scorers build the hole around it instead of trying shots they can't repeat. Example: a tight hole with out of bounds left, and you draw the ball. Tee up on the left side of the box and aim at the right rough. The ball curves back into play. [More on tee box strategy](/blogs/playing-strategies-for-golfers/3-quick-tips-to-optimizing-angles-off-the-tee).

## 3. Practice your short game

Short game is where most amateurs can save strokes fastest, at any level. Rate yourself honestly in seven areas and practice the weakest:

- Short putts, mid-range putts, lag putts
- Pitches from the fairway
- Chips from the rough
- Bump and runs
- Bunker shots

A better short game also takes pressure off your approach shots. Start with the [save-par session](/blogs/short-game-improvement-guide/short-game-practice-session-to-help-you-save-par).

## 4. Fix three mental mistakes

- **Overthinking mechanics:** one simple swing thought at most. Focus on routine and target.
- **Unrealistic expectations:** you're not supposed to pull off the recoveries you see on TV. Expecting to sets you up for frustration.
- **Watching the score:** let your partner keep the card and stay on the next shot.

## 5. Practice like you play

Put the club back in the bag after each shot. Go through your routine. Change targets constantly. Focus on distance, not just direction. [Here's why it matters](/blogs/the-blog/you-are-wasting-your-time-practicing).

## Fair warning

This takes time. Don't expect a career round next week. Enjoy the process, and keep practicing your short game.
