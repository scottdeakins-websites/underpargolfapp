Every golfer goes through it: three bad rounds in a row and suddenly every part of your game feels broken. It usually isn't. A slump is often one or two problems plus a lot of lost confidence. Here's how to get out.

## 1. Find the real problem

Don't blame everything. Look at your last few rounds and find the one or two areas actually costing you strokes: penalty shots off the tee, three-putts, missed up-and-downs. Then build your practice around those, and only those.

## 2. Play more, practice less

One relaxed round with friends at a course you love can do more than a week on the range. Or play a **slump buster**: a shorter course with flatter greens, or move up a set of tees. Seeing a good number on the card again matters.

## 3. Stop thinking about mechanics

Keep technical swing work to about **20%** of your practice time. Spend the rest hitting real shots to real targets and learning from each one. Stop every so often to write down what you noticed.

## 4. Build a highlight reel

Save video of your best swings and putts from practice and rounds, then watch it before you play. It rebuilds your rhythm and gives your mind a picture of success. Dave Stockton famously used a reel of made putts the same way.

## 5. Lean on your short game

A sharp short game covers for loose ball striking and turns OK rounds into good ones. Our [save-par short game session](/blogs/short-game-improvement-guide/short-game-practice-session-to-help-you-save-par) is the one we give players in a slump.

## 6. Take a few days off

Stepping away lets your body rest and your head clear. Use the time for fitness, stretching and some cardio. You'll come back with fewer swing thoughts.

## 7. Talk to a coach

If you've tried the first six and nothing's moved, get another set of eyes. A coach can usually spot in one round review what you've been missing for a month. That's what [mindset coaching](/pages/mindset-coaching) at Under Par is for.
