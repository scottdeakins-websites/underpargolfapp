There's no universal answer to whether a line on your ball helps. Many good players use one on short, fairly straight putts, roughly inside 10 feet, and ignore it on longer ones. The only way to know whether it helps *you* is to test it.

## Possible benefits

- Easier aim on short, straight putts.
- A visual check that the putter face is square.
- On long putts, aim is handled, so you can focus on speed.

## Possible drawbacks

- Less useful on long or big-breaking putts.
- Some players find it distracting, or spend too long lining it up.

## The "Line It Up" test session (about 50 min)

| Block | Time | Putts | What to notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Putter path drill, no line | 8 min | 18 | Settle your stroke |
| 5-footers with a line | 14 min | 27 | Straight, 3" left-to-right, 3" right-to-left |
| 10-footers with a line | 14 min | 27 | About 5" of break each way |
| 20-footers | 10 min | 18 | Backswing vs. follow-through length |
| 40-footers | 5 min | 9 | Does it free you up to focus on pace? |

For the putter path drill, lay an alignment stick parallel to a straight 5-foot putt, just outside the ball. On the breaking putts, aim the line at your start point, not the hole.

## Decide

After the session, write down whether you'll use a line, on which putts, and where it fits in your [putting routine](/blogs/short-game-improvement-guide/building-a-pre-shot-routine-for-your-short-game). Then build it into your regular [putting practice](/blogs/scratch-golfs-guide-to-putting/putting-practice-routine).
