Most golfers have a pre-shot routine for full swings and nothing for the short game. That's where strokes leak. You need three routines: **full swing, short game and putting.** Each one helps you focus, calms nerves and keeps you in the moment.

## Chipping and pitching

1. **Pick your landing spot first.** Usually on the green, so the ball doesn't bounce off the fringe, then rolls out like a putt. Sometimes you'll land it on the fringe or fairway and let it release.
2. **Practice swings beside the ball.** The opposite of your full swing, where you rehearse behind it. Find grass like your lie, so you can feel how the club will move through it.
3. **As many practice swings as you need,** usually one or two.
4. **Step in, look at the landing spot once more, and go.** No hesitation.

## Bunker shots

Practice swings can go beside or behind the ball (you can't touch the sand in the bunker). Try both in practice and keep the one that gives you more confidence.

## Putting

Putting routines vary more than any other. Two approaches to test:

- **Beside the ball:** one or two practice strokes next to it, step in and putt.
- **Behind the ball:** make practice strokes from behind while looking at the hole to feel the pace, then walk in on your line, take one last look and putt with no more practice strokes.

## Keep it quick

Faster routines tend to produce better results. [Here's why](/blogs/mindset/why-you-need-to-speed-up-your-pre-shot-routine). Test them in practice, then use them on the course, especially when the pressure's on.
