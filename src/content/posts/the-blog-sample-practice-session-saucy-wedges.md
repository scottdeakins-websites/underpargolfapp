Most of the shots in your round are played from 100 yards and in. This session trains exactly that: distance control with every wedge, from 10 to 100 yards.

## The session at a glance

| Block | Time | Balls | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Wedge warm-up | 10 min | 18 | No score |
| 2. 10x10 approach challenge | 35 min | 50 | Shots within your target window |
| 3. Weak link | 5 min | 8 | No score |

## 1. Wedge warm-up (10 min)

Three balls each, in this order:

1. Lob wedge, chip
2. Lob wedge, full
3. Sand wedge, 80%
4. Sand wedge, full
5. Gap wedge, 80%
6. Gap wedge, full

## 2. 10x10 approach challenge (35 min)

Five balls each from **10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 and 100 yards**. Pick a target and try to *carry* the ball exactly that distance.

- No good targets on your range? Mark each 10-yard interval with an alignment stick, towel or tennis ball.
- **Tiger's version:** hit one ball, then try to land the next four on that exact spot.
- **Bonus:** hit the last two balls at each distance from the rough.

**Score it:** count shots that land within 3 yards of the number inside 50 yards, and within 5 yards beyond 50. Write down each distance's score.

## 3. Weak link (5 min)

Which distance was worst? Hit eight more from there and leave on a good one.

Struggling to control a particular distance? Build your [wedge distance chart](/blogs/full-swing-guide-from-scratch-golf/how-to-manage-wedge-distances-like-a-pro) first.
