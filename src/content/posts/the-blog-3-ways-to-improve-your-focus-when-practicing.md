*By Michael Leonard, Under Par mental coach and author of Wicked Smart Golf*

Most golfers lose focus during practice. I have too. And practice without focus is mostly exercise. Phones haven't helped: we've trained ourselves to switch attention every few seconds. The good news is you can train it back. Here are three ways.

## 1. Use music to focus

- **Build a golf playlist** of songs that get you going. Rock, rap, country: whatever works.
- **Use two playlists.** One upbeat for speed training, when you want to be fired up like a long-drive hitter. One instrumental (no lyrics) or binaural beats for focused technical work.
- **Try one song on repeat.** A repeated track fades into the background and stops your mind from wandering.
- **Make it a trigger.** For me, earbuds in means it's time to work. That habit carries from my writing to the range.

## 2. Take planned breaks

Breaks reduce stress, clear your head and make the next block more productive, but only if you plan them.

- **Decide break points in advance:** after 10 balls, after half the bucket, after 20 minutes of putting.
- **Use the break:** review how the session is going and reset for the next block.
- **Try the Pomodoro method:** 25 minutes of practice, 5 minutes off.

## 3. Have a plan

Think about two gym sessions.

**Workout 1, no plan:** you don't know what you're training or for how long. You shoot some baskets, walk on the treadmill, do a few random ab exercises, scroll your phone for 45 minutes and leave no closer to your goal.

**Workout 2, a plan:** a 10-minute warm-up, then four supersets for chest and back. Fifty minutes later you're tired, and you're motivated to come back.

Golf practice works the same way. Show up with drills, reps and scores already decided. Start with the [1-hour practice session](/blogs/the-blog/best-1-hour-golf-practice-session) or read [how to build a golf practice plan](/blogs/the-blog/golf-practice-plan).
