Not throwing your club isn't the same as having a post-shot routine. Most top players have a short, consistent way of processing every shot, good or bad, before they walk to the next one.

## Why it matters

- **After good shots:** enjoy it and carry the momentum into the next swing.
- **After bad shots:** this is the important one. Figure out what happened, make a small adjustment, and stop a bad shot from turning into a bad hole.
- **Mid-round mechanics:** keep them to a minimum. Most amateurs play better with the swing they brought, and fix mechanics on the range afterward.
- **Your playing partners:** golfers who sulk, yell at themselves or throw clubs (Happy Gilmore launching the pin comes to mind) get fewer invitations. Shooter McGavin's approach is better: skip the drama, give your partners a knowing smile.

## The 10-second routine

1. **Evaluate the result without emotion.** What actually happened?
2. **Turn it into a positive instruction.** Not "don't rush your routine," but "keep going through your full routine."
3. **Picture a good outcome** for the next time.
4. **Take a deep breath.**
5. **Move on** to the next shot.

## Use a growth mindset

Every shot is information. A hook into the trees is a test of your recovery and your strategy. A plugged bunker lie is the chance to use what you practiced from [bad lies](/blogs/the-blog/practice-tip-practice-from-bad-lies).

Pair this with a solid [pre-shot routine](/blogs/mindset/a-simple-blueprint-for-your-pre-shot-routine) and you've got a full routine that holds up under pressure.
