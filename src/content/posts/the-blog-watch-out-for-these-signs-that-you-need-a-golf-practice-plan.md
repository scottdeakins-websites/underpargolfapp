Here's what most golfers' practice looks like: a few wedges to warm up, then up through the bag with no real purpose. Irons at the same flag, then drivers, then a few more random clubs. The chipping green gets skipped because there were emails to answer. If that sounds familiar, here are the signs it's time for a plan.

## 1. You practice, but your handicap doesn't move

If the time is going in and the results aren't coming out, the practice isn't matching your game.

**Fix it:** cover every part of the game each week, especially short game. Change clubs often. Hit high and low shots, draws and fades. Be honest about your weaknesses, and keep your strengths sharp too.

## 2. You spend more time on the range than the short game area

The range is more fun. But most of your shots are played from close to the green.

**Fix it:** move the majority of your time to chipping and putting. Make it engaging with games and challenges, and track your scores to add pressure. The [save-par session](/blogs/short-game-improvement-guide/short-game-practice-session-to-help-you-save-par) is a good place to start.

## 3. You don't know what you'll work on until you get there

Doing the same routine every time, without looking at your rounds, means you're practicing out of habit.

**Fix it:** review each round and write the next week's plan from it. Struggled with a ball against the collar of the green? That shot goes into this week's sessions.

## 4. You spend the last 20 minutes trying to hit the range picker

Satisfying, yes. Useful, no. If you're losing focus or getting distracted by friends, a written plan keeps you on track. More tips: [3 ways to improve your focus when practicing](/blogs/the-blog/3-ways-to-improve-your-focus-when-practicing).
