Tight hole, out of bounds right, and you reach for the 3-wood. For most mid-handicappers that's the wrong call. The 3-wood doesn't hit many more fairways, and it leaves you 20 yards further back.

The better play is a **fairway finder**: your driver, set up to take the big miss away. It takes three changes, and none of them is to your swing.

## Step 1: Tee it lower

A lower tee gives you a more penetrating flight. The ball spends less time in the air, so a slight curve has less time to become a big one, and the wind has less to work with.

## Step 2: Choke up 1–2 inches

A shorter club is easier to control, and choking up makes the shaft play a little firmer, which helps keep the ball straighter. Combined with the lower tee, it takes most of the wild misses out of play. You'll still typically carry it **10–15 yards past your 3-wood**.

## Step 3: Change nothing else

You'll stand slightly closer to the ball because the club is shorter. That's it. Same ball position, same backswing, same tempo. The biggest mistake is adding swing thoughts under pressure ("shorter swing," "move the ball back"). That's how a smooth swing turns into a steer.

| | Normal driver | Fairway finder |
|---|---|---|
| Tee height | Normal | Lower |
| Grip | Full length | 1–2 in. down |
| Distance from ball | Normal | Slightly closer |
| Swing | Normal | Normal |

## The range drill: fairway streak

1. Pick two flags on the range about 30 yards apart. That's your fairway.
2. Hit fairway finders until one misses. Count the streak.
3. Write down your best streak and try to beat it next session.

The streak matters. Every swing carries the pressure of the ones before it, which is what the 16th tee feels like.

## On the course

Use it on tight holes, when the miss is penalty trouble, and whenever you've lost trust in the big driver mid-round. Pair it with the right side of the tee box. [Here's how to pick it](/blogs/playing-strategies-for-golfers/3-quick-tips-to-optimizing-angles-off-the-tee).
