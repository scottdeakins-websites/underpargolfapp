Most three-putts aren't caused by a bad read. They're caused by a bad first putt: off the sweet spot, too firm or way short. This session works on the two things behind good speed, **centered contact** and **a consistent stroke length**.

**What you need:** an alignment stick (or a club), two tees, a coin and about 50 minutes on the practice green.

## The session at a glance

| Block | Time | Putts | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Putter path drill | 8 min | 18 | Smooth along the stick |
| 2. "Basil" coin drill | 5 min | Strokes only | Brush the coin's edge |
| 3. Tiger's gate drill | 6 min | 18 | Clean through the gate |
| 4. One-handed mid-range | 5 min | 10 | Finishes inside 3 ft |
| 5. Mid-range coin challenge | 8 min | 18 | Rolls over the coin |
| 6. Long-range coin challenge | 8 min | 15 | Rolls over the coin |
| Wrap up | 10 min | Your choice | Repeat your weakest drill |

## 1. Putter path drill (8 min)

Lay an alignment stick (or a club or flagstick) parallel to a straight 5-foot putt, just outside the ball. Hit 18 putts, keeping the putter moving smoothly along the line.

## 2. The "Basil" drill (5 min)

Place a thick coin on the green and make strokes that clip the *side* of the coin with the sole of your putter. Vary the length of your stroke. The goal is a putter that stays low and brushes the grass. This is the feel Nick Faldo leaned on during his 1992 Open win, with "Basil" as a reminder to keep the putter low.

## 3. Tiger's gate drill (6 min)

From 3 feet, set two tees just wider than your putter head. Hit 12 putts with your dominant hand only (lefties, left hand), then 6 with both hands.

## 4. One-handed mid-range putts (5 min)

From 25 feet, hit 10 putts with one hand, whichever feels more natural. The putter feels heavier with one hand, which helps your rhythm, stops you flicking the wrists and keeps your upper arm connected to your body.

## 5. Mid-range coin challenge (8 min)

Place a coin about 2 feet in front of the ball, on your start line. From 25 feet, hit three straight putts, three left-to-right and three right-to-left. Do it twice: 18 putts. **Score** how many roll over the coin for each type. You'll likely find one break suits your eye better than the other.

## 6. Long-range coin challenge (8 min)

Same setup at 40, 45 and 50 feet. One putt from each distance per circuit, five circuits, 15 putts. **Score** your hits and compare with the mid-range numbers. How much does your start line drift as the putts get longer?

## Wrap up (10 min)

Go back to whichever drill scored worst and do it again.
