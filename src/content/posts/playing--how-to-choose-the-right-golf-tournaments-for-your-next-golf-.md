*By Michael Leonard*

Casual rounds don't tell you how your game holds up under pressure. Tournaments do, whether you shoot in the 90s or the 70s. They expose weaknesses so your practice gets more efficient, introduce you to like-minded golfers, train you to hit shots under pressure, and every now and then, you win something.

I've played more than 200 tournament days since 2016, from local events to Q-school in 2019. Here's how I think about building a season: three levels of events.

## 1. "Hit and giggle" events

**What:** league nights, two- or four-person scrambles, back-nine networking events.
**Format:** one day, social, but played by the rules: no gimmes.
**Why:** an easy way into the season, useful feedback for practice and new people to play with. Perfect if you're new to competing.

## 2. Championship events

**What:** state tournaments, men's or women's club events, club championships, member-members and member-guests.
**Format:** often multi-day, higher entry fees, sometimes a cut.
**Why:** this is real tournament golf: tough conditions and real pressure.

## 3. Stretch tournaments

**What:** your majors. The events you circle months in advance and build your preparation around: a USGA qualifier, a state amateur.
**Mindset:** signing up should make you a little nervous. I felt that before my first U.S. Open qualifier and Q-school.
**Why:** it pushes you out of your comfort zone, motivates deeper practice, and makes every other event feel easier.

## Plan the mix

Pick a few of each. Every round either teaches you something or confirms your practice is working.
