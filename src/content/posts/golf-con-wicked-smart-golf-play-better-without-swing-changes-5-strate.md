*By Michael Leonard, author of Wicked Smart Golf and Under Par mental coach*

I dropped about 50 shots over four years of high school golf, and later chased professional golf, without ever having a perfect swing. Tour players are still tinkering with theirs. Great golf comes from a strong mental game, a reliable short game and smart decisions. *Wicked Smart Golf* has 111 tips across five pillars; here's one from each.

## 1. Mental game: focus on what you want

Your mind works toward whatever target you give it, and it's bad at "don't." Tell yourself "don't hit it in the water" and your attention is on the water. Acknowledge the trouble, then put all your attention on where you *want* the ball to go, and see it flying there.

## 2. Practice: every ball has a purpose

The biggest practice mistake I see is hitting balls with no target and no alignment. That's exercise, not practice. Give every ball a target and a shape, visualize it, and use your full routine. [More on practice that transfers](/blogs/the-blog/you-are-wasting-your-time-practicing).

## 3. Putting: live inside 5 feet

Putts from 2 to 5 feet have a big effect on your handicap and are one of the easiest things to improve. I spend about 80% of my putting practice there. When you trust the short ones, you putt the long ones more freely. Start with the [circle drill](/blogs/scratch-golfs-guide-to-putting/putting-practice-routine).

## 4. Scoring zone: weight forward with a wedge

Most golfers stand balanced or with their weight back when they hold a wedge, then try to help the ball up and thin it. Your wedge has 45+ degrees of loft; it'll get the ball up. Put your weight forward almost any time there's a wedge in your hands. [More on wedge distance control](/blogs/full-swing-guide-from-scratch-golf/how-to-manage-wedge-distances-like-a-pro).

## 5. Course management: club up

Take the longer club more often, especially when you're between two. Most trouble sits short of the green, because designers know golfers overestimate their distances. Even a so-so swing with more club gets you closer. [Build a yardage guide](/blogs/full-swing-guide-from-scratch-golf/building-a-yardage-guide) so you know your real numbers.
