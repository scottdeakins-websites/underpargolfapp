Tournament golf is a different game. Same swing, same clubs, but adrenaline, unfamiliar courses and a scorecard someone else is checking. These ten tips cover the week before, the day of and what to do after.

## Before the event

**1. Play a practice round.** On an unfamiliar course, learn the green speeds, how far you need to carry trouble off the tee and how the ball reacts on the greens. Try to play it a few days before so conditions are similar.

**2. Make a game plan.** The day before, go through every hole with a yardage book or satellite view. Decide which club to hit off each tee, which side of the tee box to use, and where you will and won't aim on each green. Adjust for wind, but stick to the plan, even after a bad hole.

**3. Leave your swing alone.** No overhauls the week of. Leave training aids at home and keep it to one swing thought at most. Spend range time on putting, wedges, driver accuracy and your [pre-shot routine](/blogs/mindset/a-simple-blueprint-for-your-pre-shot-routine). More on timing in [how to make swing changes](/blogs/full-swing-guide-from-scratch-golf/the-art-of-making-swing-changes).

**4. Sharpen your short game.** Tournament pins tend to be tucked near edges, so you'll need to get up and down from awkward spots. Practice flops, chips and bunker shots, ideally at the event course so you know how the sand and rough play. The [save-par session](/blogs/short-game-improvement-guide/short-game-practice-session-to-help-you-save-par) is built for this.

## On the day

**5. Arrive early.** About **75 minutes** before your tee time lets you register and run your whole warm-up without rushing.

**6. Play your own game.** Your playing partners' swings, putting and attitude are not your business. Stick to your plan.

**7. Plan food and water.** Bring water and snacks to keep your energy and focus up, especially if you're walking. In the heat, electrolyte packets or a little salt replace sodium without a sports drink's sugar.

**8. Manage adrenaline.** Adrenaline changes your distances. On full shots the ball often flies further, so consider less club. On the greens the opposite happens: nervous golfers leave putts short, so make sure they get to the hole.

## After the round

**9. Review it.** Write down your stats and notes the same day. Tournaments show you exactly what holds up under pressure and what doesn't, and that's your practice plan for the next few weeks.

**10. Book the next one.** Sign up for another event soon. A date on the calendar gives your practice a deadline.
