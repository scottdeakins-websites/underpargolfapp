Launch monitors used to be for tour players and club fitters. Now they're in garages and simulators everywhere, and the wall of numbers can be overwhelming. Here's what each one means and which ones to focus on.

## The metrics

| Metric | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Ball speed | How fast the ball leaves the face | The biggest driver of distance |
| Club speed | How fast the club is moving at impact | Your power potential |
| Smash factor | Ball speed ÷ club speed | Quality of strike; useful, but don't obsess |
| Launch angle | Vertical angle the ball leaves at | Higher = more carry, softer landing |
| Attack angle | Up or down at impact | Negative = hitting down; positive = hitting up |
| Spin rate | Backspin in rpm | Too much costs distance; too little won't hold greens |
| Spin axis / side spin | Tilt of the spin | Makes the ball curve |
| Club path | Direction the club moves through impact | Out-to-in or in-to-out |
| Face angle | Where the face points at impact | Mostly decides where the ball starts |

## The four to start with

Focus on **ball speed, launch angle, spin and dispersion.** Club fitters tend to lean on these too.

## Attack angle and path work together

With irons you want to hit down. With driver, many players gain distance by hitting slightly up. Attack angle and path interact, too: hitting down tends to shift the path left, and hitting up tends to shift it right (for a right-hander). Read them together.

## Face and path: why it curves

For a right-handed golfer, the face angle mostly sets the **start direction**, and the face relative to the path creates the **curve**.

- Face open to the path → the ball curves right (fade/slice).
- Face closed to the path → the ball curves left (draw/hook).

More in [how to hit a fade and a draw](/blogs/playing-strategies-for-golfers/mastering-the-fade-and-draw-the-basics-for-hitting-different-shot-shapes-in-golf).

## Dispersion: the most useful number of all

After enough shots, most monitors draw a **dispersion pattern** for each club: the spread left-right and long-short. Throw out the obvious mishits and look at the oval. A smaller oval means more accuracy. Knowing it tells you how much room to give hazards, and it belongs in your [yardage guide](/blogs/full-swing-guide-from-scratch-golf/building-a-yardage-guide).

## A note on range balls

Range balls often fly differently from your gamer ball. Adjust your numbers, or test with your own ball when you can.
