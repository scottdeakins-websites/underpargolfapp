High school golf coaches have the same problem every afternoon: a dozen players, one range, about 90 minutes, and a match on Thursday. The answer is **stations, a timer and a score at every station.** Everyone is busy, practice looks like competition, and you end the week with data on every player.

## The weekly rhythm

| Day | Focus | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Short game and putting | Station rotation |
| Tuesday | Full swing and wedges | Station rotation |
| Wednesday | On course | 9 holes, play a scramble or "worst ball" |
| Thursday | Match day | Pre-round routine and play |
| Friday | Qualifying or game day | Scored challenges, low score earns a lineup spot |

## A 90-minute station practice

Split the team into groups of three or four. Each station is 20 minutes; spend the first 10 minutes on a group warm-up.

| Station | Drill | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Putting | Circle drill at 3, 4, 5 ft (24 putts) | Makes |
| 2. Chipping | 5 balls each at 10, 15, 20 yds, then putt out | Up-and-downs |
| 3. Wedges | 5 balls each to 50, 75, 100 yds | Inside 15 ft or within 5 yds |
| 4. Tee shot | Fairway streak between two flags | Best streak |

Post the scores on a board. Players will compete without you asking them to.

## Games that create pressure

- **Up-and-down ladder:** players play 9 "holes" around the practice green. Lowest total wins.
- **Lag challenge:** each player gets 10 putts from 40 feet. Every putt inside a putter length earns a point.
- **Par-18 short game course:** design 9 chip-and-putt holes around the green. Par is 2.

## Tracking the roster

Keep one sheet per player with weekly station scores and match stats: fairways, greens, up-and-downs and putts. After three weeks, you'll know exactly who needs more short game work and who needs more time on the range. Our post on [how to build a golf practice plan](/blogs/the-blog/golf-practice-plan) explains how to read those numbers.
