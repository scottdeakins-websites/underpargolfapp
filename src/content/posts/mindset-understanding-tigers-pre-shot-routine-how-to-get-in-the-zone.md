*By Michael Leonard*

Tiger's driving, iron play, short game and putting all set him apart. But his greatest edge may have been his ability to get into "the zone" and find another gear when it mattered. His pre-shot routine was the doorway.

## Why routines matter in every sport

An NFL kicker takes the same number of steps, in the same time, before every kick. NBA players use the same dribbles, spins and foot placement at the free throw line. Routines block out distractions, calm nerves and focus attention on the task. In golf, Helen Alfredsson described the routine as building a cocoon around yourself. Tiger has said he sometimes got so focused he didn't remember hitting the shot.

## Tiger at his peak

Around 2000–2001, Tiger's routine was automatic. His movements were nearly identical before every shot, and reportedly varied by only fractions of a second from swing to swing. It was also relatively quick. More on why quicker is better in [why you need to speed up your pre-shot routine](/blogs/mindset/why-you-need-to-speed-up-your-pre-shot-routine).

## What a bad routine looks like

- **Greg Norman, 1996 Masters:** his routine changed noticeably from shot to shot. Nick Faldo, who won that week, later described Norman gripping and regripping the club.
- **Sergio Garcia, 2002 U.S. Open at Bethpage:** repeated regripping that frustrated fans and didn't help his game.

## Build your own

No two players have the same routine; Tiger, Rory McIlroy and Justin Thomas each have their own. But they all have one. Decide the details and repeat them until they're automatic:

| Detail | Make it fixed |
|---|---|
| Walk-in | Same number of steps to the ball |
| Breathing | Same number of breaths |
| Target | Same number of looks at the target |
| Timing | Same sequence, same pace, every shot |
