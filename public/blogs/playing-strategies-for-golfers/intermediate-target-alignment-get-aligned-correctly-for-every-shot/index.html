Intermediate Target Alignment: Get Aligned for Every Shot

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Alignment is the most overlooked fundamental in golf, and it's often where Under Par coaches start. When you're aimed wrong, your body makes compensations to get the ball to the target, and those compensations cause poor strikes and inconsistent shots. Fix the aim, and a lot of "swing problems" disappear.

The problem

On the range you can lay down alignment sticks. On the course you can't. Lining up to a target 150 yards away is hard; small errors at address become big misses downrange.

The fix: an intermediate target

Pick a spot a few feet in front of your ball, on the line to your target: a leaf, an old divot, a discolored blade of grass. Aim the clubface at that spot, then set your feet, hips and shoulders parallel to the line. Aiming at something three feet away is far easier than aiming at a flag.

Jack Nicklaus popularized this. If it worked for Jack, it's worth trying.

Range drill

  1. Pick a target about 150 yards away.
  2. Choose an intermediate target on that line, align to it, and hit.
  3. Lay a club along your toes. Is it parallel to the target line?
  4. Repeat 10 times with a different target each time.

Make it part of your pre-shot routine and it'll travel with you to the course.
