Where to Tee Up: 3 Rules for Picking the Right Side of the Tee Box

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Most golfers tee up in the middle of the box without thinking about it. That's free strategy left on the table. The tee box is often 10 yards wide, and where you stand changes how much fairway or green you're actually aiming at.

Rule 1: Tee up to fit your shot shape

Give your normal ball flight room to curve. For a right-handed golfer:

Your shot shape Tee up on the Why
Fade or slice (curves right) Right side You aim down the left, and the ball curves back into the fairway
Draw or hook (curves left) Left side You aim down the right, and the ball curves back toward the middle

Lefties, flip it.

Rule 2: Tee up on the side opposite where you want to finish

Don't aim down the middle by default. Decide which side of the fairway gives the best angle to the green, or keeps you away from trouble, then tee up on the opposite side so you're hitting across the fairway toward it. To favor the right side of the fairway, tee up on the left. To favor the left, tee up on the right.

Rule 3: On par 3s, tee up opposite the pin

You want the angle that shows you the most green. That's usually from the side of the box opposite the flag. Pin on the left? Tee up on the right. You're now hitting into the widest part of the green instead of across its narrowest edge.

Make it a habit

Before you tee up, ask two questions: Which way does my ball curve? and Which side do I want to be on? That's five seconds, and it's the easiest stroke you'll save all day. For tight holes, combine it with the fairway finder.

Common questions

Should I tee up on the right or left side if I slice?

Tee up on the right side of the box (for a right-handed golfer) and aim down the left side, so your slice curves back into the fairway instead of away from it.
