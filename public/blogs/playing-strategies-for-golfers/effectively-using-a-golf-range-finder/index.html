How to Use a Golf Rangefinder: The 3 Numbers That Matter

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Most golfers zap the flag, pick a club and swing. The flag number matters, but it's only part of the picture. Build these three numbers into your pre-shot routine.

1. Flag distance

Your starting point for club selection.

2. Cover distance

The yardage to the front edge of the green: what you must carry to land on the putting surface. Most important when a bunker guards the front. If your stock club only just covers it, take one more.

3. Room left and right

If the course has pin sheets, know how many yards there are on each side of the hole. When the pin is tucked in a corner, treat it as a red light: aim for the center of the green.

Situation Play
Front bunker, front pin Carry the cover number with margin; accept a longer putt
Pin tucked on one side Aim at the middle of the green
Lots of green around the pin Green light: aim at it

Play your misses

Greg Norman once said he hit maybe one perfect shot a round. Golf is a game of misses, so aim where your likely miss still finishes somewhere good. Know which way your ball curves (see where to tee up) and give it room.

For big events: a yardage book

For your home course or a big tournament, a yardage and green-reading book is the ultimate cheat sheet. Rules on detailed green-reading books have tightened at the top level, so check your event's local rules first. Need your own club distances? Build a yardage guide.
