Why PGA Tour Golfers Turn to Statisticians

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

More and more tour players have a statistician on their team alongside a swing coach, caddie and trainer. Here's what they actually do, and how you can borrow the idea.

1. Find the real weak spots

Statisticians look for patterns across many rounds: club selection, approach play, putting by distance. If a player keeps losing strokes on long lag putts, that becomes the practice priority.

2. Plan around typical misses

If a player misses left on approach shots more often than not, the statistician might suggest checking alignment, or changing targets: aim at the center when the pin is tucked left, and play more aggressively when it's on the right.

3. Prepare for each course

Before a tournament, they match hole lengths, green slopes and hazard placement to the player's strengths, then recommend a club and target line for each hole.

4. Be a sounding board

During tournament week, a good analyst helps the player think through decisions and offers a fresh view, especially when things aren't going well.

5. Provide an objective view

Golfers are hard on themselves and miss their own strengths. An outside analyst sees the numbers without the emotion.

Does it work?

Results vary by player, but most tour players clearly see value in it. Clear, data-based plans replace gut feel, and that tends to produce more confident, consistent golf.

The amateur version

You don't need a statistician. You need:

  1. Your stats. Start tracking your golf stats.
