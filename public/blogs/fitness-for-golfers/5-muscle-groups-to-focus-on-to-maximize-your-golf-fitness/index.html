The 5 Muscle Groups Golfers Should Train (With Exercises)

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

You don't need to be a bodybuilder to hit it further. But a stronger, more mobile body makes a better swing easier to repeat, adds speed, and keeps you playing without your back complaining. These are the five muscle groups that matter most for golf.

Check with a doctor or physical therapist before starting a new strength program, especially if you have an injury.

The five at a glance

Muscle group Why it matters Exercises
Core Posture and balance through the swing Planks, Russian twists, bird dogs
Glutes Your main power source Squats, deadlifts, lunges, glute bridges
Back Posture and power Rows, lat pull-downs
Shoulders Stability and control Shoulder presses, lateral raises, rotator cuff work
Forearms Grip and clubface control Wrist curls, wrist extensions, grip training

1. Core

Your core holds your posture steady while your body rotates at speed. A weak core is why spine angle changes mid-swing and contact suffers. Planks, Russian twists and bird dogs build stability without heavy equipment.

2. Glutes

The glutes are where a lot of your swing speed comes from: they drive the hips and stabilize your lower body as you rotate. Train them to be strong and stable with squats, deadlifts, lunges and glute bridges.

3. Back

A strong back helps you hold your spine angle and turn powerfully. Rows and lat pull-downs are the basics. They also balance out all the hunched-over time most of us spend at desks.

4. Shoulders

Your shoulders guide the club, so they need strength and range of motion. Shoulder presses and lateral raises build strength; rotator cuff exercises with a light band keep the joint healthy.

5. Forearms

Grip strength affects clubface control, and it matters most out of rough. Wrist curls, wrist extensions and grip training take a few minutes and can be done at home.

Putting it into your week

Start simple: add one or two of these exercises to your routine each day, rotating through all five groups across the week. Once that's a habit, move to two or three full sessions a week. The off-season is the best time to build up. See the winter practice plan.

Common questions

What muscles are most important for golf?

The core, glutes, back, shoulders and forearms. The glutes and core drive power and stability; the back and shoulders support posture and control; the forearms hold the club.

How often should golfers strength train?

Two to three sessions a week is a good starting point, plus a few minutes of mobility work most days.
