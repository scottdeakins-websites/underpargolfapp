Go Low: An 80-Minute Wedge Practice Session

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Good wedge play gives you the confidence to attack pins, more birdie chances and more par saves when you miss a green. This session is a big one: 100 wedge shots.

Mindset: perseverance is rarely wasted. Setbacks will come today, on the course and off it. How will you respond?

The session at a glance

Block Time Balls Score
1. Wedge warm-up 5 min 10 No score
2. 10x10 approach 60 min 100 Within 5 yds of the number
3. Drivers 6 min 10 In a 20-yard fairway
4. Circle drill 10 min 24 Makes out of 24

1. Warm-up (5 min)

5 sand wedges at about 80%, then 5 full.

2. 10x10 approach (60 min)

Ten balls each from 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100, 110 and 120 yards. Pick a target and try to carry it the exact distance. Mark 10-yard intervals with an alignment stick or tennis ball if your range doesn't have targets.

Write down the club you used at every distance. That becomes your wedge distance chart.

3. Drivers (6 min)

Mark a 20-yard-wide fairway. Ten drives, count how many land in it. Good drives set up the wedges you just practiced.

4. Circle drill (10 min)

Three balls at 2, 3 and 4 feet, repeated at eight spots around the hole. 24 putts; count your makes.

Shorter on time? The Saucy Wedges session covers 10–100 yards in 50 minutes.
