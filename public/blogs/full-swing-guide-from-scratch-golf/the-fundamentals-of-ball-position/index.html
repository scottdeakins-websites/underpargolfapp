Golf Ball Position: Where to Play Every Club, Driver to Wedge

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Ball position is the cheapest fix in golf. It takes no extra swing speed and no new move, and it changes how solidly you hit it, how high it launches and where it starts. Tour players check it constantly, usually with an alignment stick on the ground. Most amateurs never check it at all.

Here's where the ball goes for every club, and how to make it automatic.

The ball position chart

Positions are for a right-handed golfer. Lefties, mirror everything: your lead heel is your right heel.

Club Ball position What it does
Driver Inside of the lead heel Lets you hit up on it: higher launch, less spin, more distance
Fairway woods About 2 in. inside the lead heel Shallow, sweeping strike off the turf
Long irons (2–4) About 2 in. inside the lead heel Clean contact with a slight downward strike
Mid irons (5–7) About 1 in. ahead of center Ball first, then turf
Short irons (8–9) and wedges Center of the stance Steeper, descending strike for spin and control

The pattern is simple. As the club gets shorter and the loft goes up, the ball moves back. That moves the low point of your swing in front of the ball, so you hit down on your irons and up on your driver.

Ball position for irons

This is the one most golfers get wrong. If you play every iron from the same spot, you'll hit your short irons fat or thin and your long irons weak to the right.

Use the chart above as a starting point, then watch your divots:

  • Divot starts behind the ball: the ball is too far forward. Move it back half a ball.
  • No divot, thin contact: the ball is probably too far back, or you're hanging back. Move it up slightly.
  • Divot starts just after the ball and points at the target: that's where you want it.

How to check it every time

Build the check into your pre-shot routine:

  1. Stand behind the ball, pick a target and see the shot.
  2. Set up, then use the clubhead to confirm the ball sits where it should for that club.
  3. Once a week, film your setup from face-on or check it in a mirror. Most golfers are surprised by what video shows.

Two drills that make it stick

The tee drill. Push a tee into the ground at the correct spot for the club you're hitting, right beside the ball. Hit 10 shots with each club in your bag that day. You're training your eyes as much as your setup.

The alignment stick drill. Lay one stick on your target line and a second stick perpendicular to it, pointing at the ball. Move the second stick as you change clubs. This is how tour players practice, and it takes about 30 seconds to set up.

Where this fits in practice

Ball position is a five-minute warm-up check, not a whole session. Pair it with a structured session like our 1-hour practice plan so you're checking it on every club, not just the 7-iron.

Common questions

Where should the ball be for irons?

Long irons (2–4) go about two inches inside your lead heel. Mid irons (5–7) go about an inch ahead of the center of your stance. Short irons and wedges go in the center.

Where should the ball be for driver?

Opposite the inside of your lead heel. That forward position helps you hit up on the ball for higher launch and less spin.

Should ball position change for left-handed golfers?

Use the same positions, mirrored. Your lead heel is your right heel.
