How to Hit It Further: 5 Ways to Hit Bombs Off the Tee

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Distance has exploded on tour. In 2022, for the first time, most PGA Tour players averaged more than 300 yards off the tee. John Daly was the first to average over 300, back in 1997. Bombs are fun, they impress your playing partners, and when you control them they lower your scores. Here are five ways to add some.

1. Tee it higher, ball forward

Tee the ball a little higher and move it slightly forward in your stance. That tilts your shoulders away from the target (lead shoulder higher), which helps you hit up on the ball for higher launch and less spin.

2. Lighten your grip

When we try to hit it hard, we squeeze. That tension restricts your wrist hinge and the release of the club, and it costs speed. Aim for medium pressure: firm enough to control the club, loose enough for the wrists to work.

3. Take hard practice swings

Bryson DeChambeau's practice swings are faster than most golfers' real swings. Before your drive, make a couple of fast practice swings and listen for a loud whoosh low and in front of the ball. Train speed, and your body learns it.

4. Train your legs

Rory McIlroy and Cameron Champ aren't big guys, but they produce elite speed through mechanics and strength. Amateurs tend to train their arms and chest; the power comes from the ground up.

5. Get help when power disappears

If your distance suddenly drops or your driving gets erratic, don't spiral through YouTube fixes. A coach can usually find the cause on video quickly. That's part of swing coaching.
