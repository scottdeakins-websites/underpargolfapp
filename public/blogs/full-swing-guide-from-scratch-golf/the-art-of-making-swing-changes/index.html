How to Make a Swing Change Without Wrecking Your Game

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Every swing change is a bet. It might get worse before it gets better, and sometimes it just gets worse. But your swing is always changing anyway, so the real skill is knowing when to change it and how to do it without losing the season.

Rule 1: Keep what works

When you're playing well, write it down. After good sessions, put a few lines in your phone notes: the swing thought, the feel, the ball flight. Video shows you what a swing looked like. Notes tell you what it felt like, and that's what you'll need when an old fault comes back in July.

Rule 2: Get a second set of eyes

A coach can find the real cause of a miss, give you the right drill and check your progress between sessions. But a coach only helps if you practice in between. If you're working on your own:

  • Picture the shot before every swing.
  • Make the new move at half to three-quarter speed first.
  • Start with short irons and work up to longer clubs.
  • Stop every 10–15 balls and ask what's working.

Rule 3: Make big changes in the off-season

Winter is for technique. You're not chasing a score, so you can live with ugly shots. Don't stop playing, though. Take the change to the course in casual rounds. Some changes only click on the course, and the course shows you what needs more range time. One swing thought at a time.

Rule 4: Never overhaul before a tournament

Big changes the week of an event fill your head with mechanics just when you need to be thinking about targets. Tournament week is for fundamentals (grip, tempo, alignment, ball position) and for short game, putting and your routine.

Rule 5: Be patient

You'll have bad sessions. When it stalls, remember why you started the change. Commit to the process long enough to find out if it works.

The swing change timeline

When What to do
Off-season Make the change. Technical practice, slow reps, casual rounds.
Early season Refine it. One swing thought at a time.
Tournament week Fundamentals only. Short game and routine.
All year Keep notes on your best swing thoughts.

Not sure the change is worth making? That's exactly what swing coaching is for: your coach works with the swing you have and changes only what's costing you shots.

Common questions

When is the best time to make a golf swing change?

The off-season. You can focus on technique without worrying about scores. Keep testing it in casual rounds so it carries to the course.

Should I change my swing before a tournament?

No. In tournament week, stick to fundamentals like grip, tempo, alignment and ball position, and spend your time on short game, putting and routine.
