How to Hit a Flighted Wedge: 5 Steps to a Knockdown That Stops

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

A full wedge feels like the right shot from 110 yards. It usually isn't. Full swings make more spin and more height, and they make your distance harder to repeat. A flighted wedge (also called a knockdown) uses more club and less swing. It flies lower, holds its line in the wind and stops close to where it lands.

Good players hit this shot most of the time. Here's how.

The 5 steps

1. Club up and choke down. Take one more club than the number says. If your gap wedge goes 120, hit pitching wedge. Grip down about an inch to take a few yards off.

2. Narrow your stance. Bring your feet about an inch closer together than a normal wedge setup.

3. Move the ball back slightly. One to two inches back from where you'd normally play it. Don't go so far back that it's off your trail foot. That's where chunks and pushes come from.

4. Shorten the backswing. Think 80%. Stop the club well short of parallel.

5. Keep the hands low in the finish. Finish with your hands below your ribcage. This keeps your hands ahead of the ball at impact, so you compress it instead of scooping it.

Club Normal One more
Grip Full length About 1 in. down
Ball position Normal 1–2 in. back
Backswing Full About 80%
Finish High Hands below the ribs
Flight High, more spin Lower, less spin, easier to repeat

The drill: learn your numbers

On the range, hit five flighted shots with each wedge using all five steps. Write down carry and total distance for each club. If you have access to a launch monitor or simulator, compare spin to your full wedges. You'll usually see a big drop.

Those numbers become part of your wedge distance chart, so on the course you're choosing a shot you've measured, not guessing.

When to use it

  • Into the wind. Less height and spin means the wind has less to grab.
  • Firm or sloped greens. When too much backspin would pull the ball off the front.
  • Most of the time. Once you trust it, it's the default shot from 80–130 yards. Save full wedges for when you need maximum height.

Common questions

What is a flighted wedge?

A wedge hit with less than a full swing and more club, so it flies lower, spins less and is easier to control, especially in the wind.

How far back should the ball be for a knockdown wedge?

About one to two inches back from your normal wedge position. Any further and you'll get steep, chunked or pushed shots.
