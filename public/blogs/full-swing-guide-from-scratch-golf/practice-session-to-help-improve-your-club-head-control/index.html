Club Head Control: A 60-Minute Shot-Shaping Practice Session

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

This is a ball-striking session built around controlling the club head: hitting down through the ball, flighting it low, and curving it both ways. It's also one of the most fun sessions we have.

Mindset: Tiger once said he smiles at obstacles. Today, notice how you react when a shot doesn't come off.

The session at a glance

Block Time Balls Score
1. Full swing warm-up 15 min 29 No score
2. Tee impact drill 10 min 10 Tees broken
3. Stingers 12 min 18 Shots under the target height
4. Nine-shot drill: iron 12 min 18 Shots that came off as called
5. Nine-shot drill: driver 12 min 18 Shots that came off as called

1. Warm-up (15 min)

Use the pre-round warm-up sequence: wedges at 50%, then full wedges, 8-irons and 5-irons at different targets, a few fairway woods and drivers at a 15-yard fairway, and three first-tee shots.

2. Tee impact drill (10 min)

Push a tee into the ground about half an inch in front of the ball (on a mat, use a ball marker). Hit 10 irons, trying to clip the tee every time. It forces you to swing down and through: ball first, then turf. Your divot should start at the ball and run through where the tee was.

3. Stingers (12 min)

Two circuits of: 3 × 5-iron, 3 × hybrid or driving iron, 3 × 3-wood.

  • Ball slightly back in your stance.
  • Feel your trail shoulder "covering" the ball through impact.
  • Short, held finish.

Pick a height you want to stay under (a tree line, a sign) and count the shots that stay below it. You can try it with driver, though even Tiger rarely did.

4–5. The nine-shot drill (12 min each)

With an iron, then with driver: straight, fade and draw, each at normal, high and low trajectory. Two sets of nine.

Call each shot before you hit it and count how many come off. Then answer two questions in your notes: Which shot do I trust most today? Which one should I work on? For the setup changes, see how to hit a fade and a draw.
