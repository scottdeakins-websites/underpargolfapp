A Putting Practice Routine That Fixes Three-Putts

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Most golfers practice putting by dropping three balls and stroking them at a hole. It feels productive, but it doesn't train anything. A good putting routine works on the three skills that make putts, in order:

  1. Start line: can you roll it where you're aimed?
  2. Speed: can you control how far it goes?
  3. Pressure: can you do both when it counts?

This routine takes about 40 minutes. Bring two tees, a coin and an alignment stick or a spare club.

Part 1: Start line (10 min)

Gate drill. From 3 feet, push two tees into the green just wider than your putter head. Hit 12 putts with your dominant hand only, then 6 with both. If you clip a tee, your face or path is off.

Path drill. Lay an alignment stick just outside a straight 5-foot putt and hit 18 putts, keeping the putter moving smoothly along it.

Part 2: Speed (18 min)

Coin challenge. Put a coin 2 feet in front of the ball on your start line. From 25 feet, hit three straight putts, three breaking left and three breaking right. Twice through. Rolling the ball over the coin from long range trains the start line and the pace that controls distance.

Ladder drill. Putt one ball each to 20, 30, 40 and 50 feet, then back down. Three circuits. Score how many finish inside 3 feet. Every miss is a three-putt waiting to happen.

Part 3: Pressure (12 min)

Circle drill, in a row. Eight balls around the hole at 3 feet. Make all eight, then move to 4 feet, then 5. Miss one and you start that distance again. Write down how far you got.

Common questions

How long should I practice putting?

Thirty to forty minutes, two or three times a week, is plenty if every putt has a purpose and a score.

What should I practice first, short putts or long putts?

Start with start line and contact on short putts, then speed on long putts, then finish with a pressure game so the session ends with putts that matter.
