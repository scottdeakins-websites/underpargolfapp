Short Game Practice Routine: The Save-Par Session

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Good rounds aren't built on perfect ball striking. They're built on saving par when you miss the green. Dr. Bob Rotella makes this point all the time: the best scorers expect to hit bad shots, accept them, and still trust themselves to get up and down.

This session is built around that idea. It's long (about 84 minutes), and you'll score every block so you know which part of your short game is leaking shots.

The session at a glance

Before you start: the mindset

Expect misses. You'll chunk a chip and blade a bunker shot. The goal today isn't perfect shots. It's a better average shot. Write down every score, good or bad, and move on to the next ball.

1. Chipping from the rough (30 min)

Hit five chips each from 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 yards, all from the rough. Do the circuit twice, 50 chips total. Score how many finish inside 6 feet: that's a putt you'll make most of the time.

2. Pitching from the fairway (18 min)

Same five distances, three balls each, from a fairway lie. Twice through for 30 shots. Score how many finish inside 10 feet. For a cleaner landing spot, try our Landing Zone session.

3. Bunker (10 min)

Two shots each from 10, 15, 20 and 25 yards, twice through for 16 shots. Score: out, on the green and inside 15 feet. Getting out every time is the first job. Getting close is the bonus.

4. Tiger's gate drill (6 min)

From 3 feet, set two tees just wider than your putter head to make a gate. Hit 12 putts with your dominant hand only (lefties, left hand), then 6 with both hands. Score how many go through clean without touching a tee. It trains a square face and a centered strike, which is what makes the short putts after your chips go in.

5. Lag putting (20 min)

Three putts each from 30, 40, 50 and 60 feet, finishing your stroke every time. Twice through for 24 putts. Score how many finish inside 3 feet. A perfect circuit is 12 of 12, and every one that misses is a likely three-putt on the course.

Track your up-and-down rate

Add your chipping and pitching scores and divide by total shots. That's your practice up-and-down rate. Do this session every two weeks and that one number will tell you if your short game is getting better.

Common questions

How long should a short game practice session be?

This one takes about 84 minutes. If you only have 40, do one circuit of each block instead of two.

What is a good up-and-down percentage?

Track it before you compare. Score every chip and pitch here, then compare against your own number from last week.
