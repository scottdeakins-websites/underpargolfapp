Why You Need to Speed Up Your Pre-Shot Routine

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

The conventional wisdom says slow down and take your time. The evidence points the other way: a quicker, consistent routine tends to produce better golf, and it helps with slow play, which nobody enjoys.

What the research found

A 2017 study of European Tour players looked at more than 22,000 shots from 47 players across five events. Players who spent less, and more consistent, time over the ball were significantly more likely to make the cut, and those who spent less time over putts were more likely to gain strokes on the field. The researchers estimated that shaving time over the ball could be worth a meaningful amount of prize money over a season.

Why amateurs slow down

Standing over the ball longer gives doubt room to grow. You start second-guessing the club, the line, your grip. The longer you wait, the less committed the swing.

Tiger's timing

At his peak in the early 2000s, Tiger's routine was automatic and lasted roughly 7.5 to 8.5 seconds. As his game struggled in later years, it got longer and less consistent.

Time your own routine

  1. Have a friend time your routine with a stopwatch, from the moment you walk into the shot to the swing.
  2. Aim for 6–10 seconds.
  3. Experiment on the range and putting green and see how timing affects results.
  4. In practice, keep it within 1–2 seconds shot to shot.
  5. Watch for a pattern: do your bad shots follow your longest routines?

Thinking time vs. routine time

Some shots need longer to assess: an odd lie, wind, a tricky green. That's fine. But once you've decided, the routine itself (step in, set up, swing) should take the same time every shot.
