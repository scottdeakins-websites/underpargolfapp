A Simple Pre-Shot Routine for Golf (4 Steps)

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

A pre-shot routine isn't a superstition. It's how you make the important decisions before you stand over the ball, so the only thing left to do is swing. The best routines are short, simple and identical every time.

Start with these four steps.

The 4-step routine

Step What you do What it's for
1. Practice swing One rehearsal swing with a single feel or cue Tells your body what's coming
2. Pick a target A specific spot, not "the fairway" Gives your mind something to aim at
3. Visualize See the shot fly and land Commits you to a shape and height
4. Commit Step in and swing. No second-guessing Stops the doubt that ruins swings

Make it yours

Use the four steps as your starting point and adjust them on the course. A few things that help:

  • Use an intermediate target. Pick a mark a few feet in front of the ball on your target line and align to that. It's far easier than aiming at a flag 150 yards away. More on this in intermediate target alignment.
  • Add a trigger. A deep breath, a waggle or a re-grip that tells you "now." It should be the same every time.
  • Practice it on the range. Do your full routine on at least the last 10 balls of every session.

Check it after every round

Ask yourself how many shots you hit without your routine. Most bad holes start with one rushed shot. Tracking it is part of our mindset coaching.

Common questions

How long should a pre-shot routine take?

Short enough that you can repeat it identically on every shot. Most good routines take well under 30 seconds from behind the ball to the swing.
