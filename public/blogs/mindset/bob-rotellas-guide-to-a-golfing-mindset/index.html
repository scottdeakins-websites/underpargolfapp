Bob Rotella's Guide to a Golfing Mindset

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Dr. Bob Rotella has worked with more major champions than almost any mental coach in golf, and his core idea is simple: build your swing on the practice tee, then trust it on the course. Those are two separate skills. Most amateurs practice the first and never practice the second.

Why Rotella

His books are easy to read, full of stories from the game, and his philosophy is uncomplicated. That simplicity is a big part of why so many tour players work with him.

Three books to start with

Book Read it for
Golf Is Not a Game of Perfect Accepting misses and scoring anyway
Golf Is a Game of Confidence Building belief that holds up under pressure
How Champions Think The mindset of top performers in and beyond sport

Putting it into practice

  • Practice mode vs. play mode. On the range, work on mechanics. On the course, one target and one commitment per shot. No technical thoughts.
  • Expect bad shots. Rotella's point is that great scorers aren't perfect; they just accept the miss and get on with the next shot. That's the idea behind our save-par short game session.
  • Train trust. Finish every range session with 10 balls in "play mode": full routine, new target each shot, no swing thoughts.

Better golf starts with how you think about yourself on the course. More in mindset coaching.
