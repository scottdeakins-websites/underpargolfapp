5 Things I Learned Working With a Sports Psychologist

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

By Michael Leonard, Under Par mental coach and author of Wicked Smart Golf

In 2019 I entered the Korn Ferry Tour Q-school pre-qualifier as one of only a few amateurs, against players for whom it was their living. To prepare, I hired sports psychologist Jim Afremow, author of The Champion's Mind. Gary Player liked to say we create our success or failure on the course mostly with our thoughts. Here's what I learned.

1. Reframe your failures

I carried painful memories of big events that went wrong. Jim had me see them differently: not as defining moments, but as proof I was mentally tough enough to still be in the fight. When a bad shot, round or tournament lingers, reframe it as something that made you tougher.

2. Control your self-talk

Most golfers believe beating themselves up helps. It almost never does. My rule now: don't say anything to yourself that you wouldn't say to a playing partner.

3. Get curious, not furious

Jim warned me the tension at Q-school would be intense. After a bad shot: no club slamming, no sulking. Ask what the shot can teach you, then move on. A post-shot routine helps.

4. Use one non-technical swing thought

Many golfers never reach their potential because they're thinking about mechanics instead of playing the course. Jim's tour clients use one swing thought at most, ideally not mechanical. Mine at Q-school: I have the smoothest tempo in the world. I used it in my pre-shot routine and as an anchor all week. Train your thought on the range first, so you trust it on the course.

5. Choose optimism

You can't control the weather, the bounces or your playing partners. You can choose your attitude every day. Payne Stewart put it well: a bad attitude is worse than a bad swing.

How it went

I missed advancing to the next stage by two shots, but I'm proud of how I handled 54 walking holes in the 90s with 90% humidity. My mental game held up. You can't control much in golf, but you can control your mind, and you can train it like anything else. See mindset coaching.
