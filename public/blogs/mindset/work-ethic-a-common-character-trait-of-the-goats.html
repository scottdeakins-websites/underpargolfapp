Tiger Woods' Work Ethic and What the GOATs Have in Common

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

The greatest athletes are remembered for their talent. Ask them, and most will tell you it was the work. Here's what four GOATs said about effort, and how to use it in your own game.

Tiger Woods

Tiger has often said he was never the most talented, biggest, fastest or strongest kid, and that it was his work ethic that carried him. In his Hall of Fame speech he made the same point: you have to earn success, and without the effort you won't get the results or deserve them.

Michael Jordan

Jordan believed half-hearted effort gets half-hearted results. Teammates said he practiced as intensely as he played in games, and the six titles and six Finals MVPs followed. He has brought the same competitiveness to golf.

Wayne Gretzky

Told as a kid that he was too small, Gretzky went on to win four Stanley Cups and set records that still stand in the NHL. He has said he wants to be remembered for working hard every day, and he credits his grandparents, who were farmers, for that grit.

Babe Ruth

Ruth was the home run king, and when he retired he also held the strikeout record. He was one of the first team athletes to hire a personal trainer, worked hard in the off-season and kept playing exhibition games long after the season ended.

What this means for your golf

Talent sets the starting line. Consistent, deliberate work decides how far you go. You don't need eight hours a day. You need:

  1. A clear goal. Break 90, break 80, a club championship.
  2. A weekly plan that fits real life. Family, work and fitness compete for the same hours, so put practice on the calendar like a meeting.
  3. A daily question: what's one thing I can do today to get closer?
