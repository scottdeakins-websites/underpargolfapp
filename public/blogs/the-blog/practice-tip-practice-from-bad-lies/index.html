Practice Tip: Practice From Bad Lies

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Most practice happens from perfect lies: a pristine range mat, a freshly raked bunker, a fluffy lie by the chipping green. The course isn't so kind. Matted rough, footprints in bunkers and hardpan around the green are where scores get away from you, and almost nobody practices them.

Next session, put the ball in a bad spot on purpose.

5 bad lies to practice

Lie What to work on
In a fairway divot Ball back slightly, hit down firmly, accept a lower flight
Plugged in a bunker Square or slightly closed face, steep swing, expect lots of roll
Against the collar of the rough Putter, hybrid or a bladed wedge on the equator of the ball
Below your feet in the rough Flex more at the knees, stay down, expect it to fly right (for a right-hander)
On a downslope in a bunker Shoulders with the slope, swing down the slope, lower flight

How to fit it in

Add five balls from one bad lie to every short game session, rotating through the list each week. Score it like any other drill: how many finished on the green, or inside 15 feet.

These are fun shots to practice, and the first time you save par from a plugged lie in a round, you'll be glad you did. They also slot easily into the save-par short game session.
