What Ben Hogan's Practice Habits Can Teach You

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Ben Hogan is on almost every list of the best ball strikers who ever lived. Jack Nicklaus reportedly ranked him above Tiger Woods in that department. Yet Hogan's swing wasn't the one other pros copied. Plenty of them thought Sam Snead had the prettier move.

What set Hogan apart was the range. He famously said the secret was in the dirt, and that he dug it out himself, through hours of hitting balls with purpose. Here are three lessons amateurs can take from it.

1. Keep practicing when you're playing well

The story goes that after building a big lead in a round, Hogan headed straight to the range instead of the clubhouse. Most of us do the opposite: a good round earns a drink, a bad round earns a bucket.

Try this: after your next good round, spend 20 minutes on the range. Write down what felt right (your swing thought, your tempo, your setup) so you can find it again when it goes missing.

2. Own your swing

Hogan didn't chase a model swing. He built a motion he trusted through repetition. Unorthodox swings win on tour every year. A coach can help you refine yours, but no coach replaces the reps.

Try this: pick one thing to improve this month and leave the rest alone. Our guide to making swing changes explains when it's worth it.

3. Practice with focus

Hogan was known to practice at the far end of the range, away from conversations and distractions. You can build the same bubble:

  • Bring a plan. Know your drills, reps and scores before you arrive. Start with the 1-hour practice session.
  • Put your phone away, except to record scores or video.
  • Wear headphones. Even without music, they tell everyone you're working.

The takeaway

Hogan didn't have a secret. He had a plan and the discipline to stick to it. You don't need eight hours a day; you need focused, scored sessions that build on each other. Here's how to build a golf practice plan.
