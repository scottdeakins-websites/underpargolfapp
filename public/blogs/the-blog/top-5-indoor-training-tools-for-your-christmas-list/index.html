5 Indoor Golf Training Tools Worth Owning

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Bad weather doesn't have to mean no golf. These five tools let you keep training at home through the winter, and they make good gifts for the golfer in your life. Prices were checked when this was first written and may have changed.

SuperSpeed sticks $199 Overspeed training for swing speed
A good golf book $16 Staying connected to the game

1. A putting mat

A quality mat like the Perfect Practice mat has printed lines for alignment, stroke length and putter path. The standard version includes a smaller cup, so the real hole looks enormous when you get back on the course. Use it for the gate and start-line drills in our putting practice routine.

2. A budget putting trainer

The PuttOut costs a fraction of a full mat. Add your own alignment and distance lines with masking tape. One warning: it needs a firm floor. Putting across thick, shaggy carpet is closer to putting out of a bunker.

3. An impact bag

Hit the bag and hold your finish to train the position that matters most: hands ahead, shaft leaning forward, body rotating through. Use it in a garage or basement and check your ceiling height first. Bonus: it's a good place to take out a bad round.

4. Speed sticks

Speed training is based on a simple idea: more clubhead speed, more distance. Overspeed sticks like SuperSpeed are designed around short, regular sessions a few days a week. Follow the manufacturer's protocol, warm up properly and stop if anything hurts.

5. A golf book

Reading about the game keeps you connected to it when you can't play. Start with The Greatest Game Ever Played by Mark Frost.

One thing to avoid

Don't spend the winter watching swing tips on YouTube and fixating on your trail elbow. Use indoor time to build confidence and strength, and to recharge. Then follow a plan: here's our winter golf practice plan.
