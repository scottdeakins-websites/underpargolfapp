How to Build a Golf Practice Plan That Lowers Your Scores

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 3 min read

Most golfers practice the same way every time: grab a bucket, hit drivers until it feels good, maybe a few chips. That's not practice, it's a warm-up with no round attached. A golf practice plan answers three questions before you leave the house: what am I working on, how much, and how will I know if it worked?

Here's how to build one in five steps, plus ready-made plans if you'd rather start today.

Step 1: Find where you're losing strokes

Before you plan practice, look at your last five rounds. If you don't track stats yet, start with these four per round:

Stat What it tells you
Fairways hit Whether the tee shot is putting you in trouble
Greens in regulation Approach and iron play
Up-and-downs Short game around the green
Putts Speed control and short putts

Look for the one or two numbers that stand out. A golfer averaging 38 putts and a golfer losing two balls a round need completely different weeks. Not sure how to read it? Our post on tracking golf stats walks through it.

Step 2: Set a realistic weekly schedule

Be honest about time. A plan you can't keep is worse than no plan. Pick the number of sessions you can actually do, and how long:

You have Try this
1 session a week One 1-hour session, rotating the focus each week
2 sessions a week One short game and putting day, one full-swing day
3–4 sessions a week The 4-day sample plan
30 minutes at a time The 30-minute practice plan

Step 3: Split your time by where scores are made

Most of the shots in a round happen within 100 yards of the hole. A good default split:

  • About 50%: short game and putting
  • About 30%: wedges and approach shots
  • About 20%: driver and long clubs

Then move time toward your weakest stat from Step 1. If you're breaking 90 for the first time, the split looks different again. See the break 90 plan and the break 80 plan.

Step 4: Score every session

Every drill needs a number: makes out of 24, shots inside 6 feet, longest fairway streak. Write it down. Scores do two things: they add a little pressure, which makes practice more like the course, and they show you whether you're improving. Every session we publish comes with a scorecard for this reason.

Step 5: Review and adjust every two weeks

Compare your practice scores and your round stats every two weeks. If your up-and-down rate is climbing, keep going. If a number hasn't moved in a month, change the drill, not the goal.

Common questions

How often should I practice golf?

Two focused sessions a week plus one round will move most golfers forward. Consistency matters more than volume: two scored sessions beat five buckets of drivers.

How should I split my practice time?

Start with about half on short game and putting, a third on wedges and approach shots, and the rest on driver and long irons. Then shift time toward whatever your stats say is costing you the most.
