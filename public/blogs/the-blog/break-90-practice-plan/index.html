Break 90 Practice Plan: A 4-Week Program

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Breaking 90 isn't about making birdies. Shoot bogey on every hole and you'll card a 90. Make a few pars and you're under. The rounds that stay above 90 are the ones with triples, lost balls and four-putts.

So this plan does three things: keep the ball in play off the tee, build one go-to shot you trust, and stop wasting shots around the green.

What to focus on

Area Goal for breaking 90
Tee shot In play, not perfect. No penalty strokes.
Approach One shot you trust from 100–150 yards
Short game On the green in one from just off it
Putting No three-putts from inside 30 feet

The weekly schedule

Two practice sessions and one round a week. About 2½ hours of practice.

Day Session Time
Session 1 Short game and putting 75 min
Session 2 Tee shot and go-to shot 60 min
Weekend Play 18, track penalty strokes, three-putts and doubles —

Week 1–2: Stop the big numbers

Session 1: Short game and putting (75 min)

  • Chipping (25 min): 5 chips each from 10, 15, 20 and 25 yards, twice through. Score: on the green and inside 10 feet.
  • Lag putting (25 min): 3 putts each from 20, 30 and 40 feet, three circuits. Score: inside 3 feet.
  • Short putts (25 min): The circle drill from the 1-hour practice session. Count makes out of 24.

Session 2: Tee shot (60 min)

  • Fairway finder (30 min): Set up the fairway finder: driver teed lower and choked down. Hit to a 30-yard "fairway" between two flags. Score: your longest streak.
  • Pick your go-to club (30 min): Hit 10 balls each with a 7-iron, 8-iron and hybrid at a flag 140–150 yards out. The club with the most shots inside 15 yards is your go-to shot.

Week 3–4: Build the go-to shot

Session 1: Same as weeks 1–2. Try to beat your scores.

Session 2: Go-to shot and wedges (60 min)

  • Go-to shot (30 min): 20 shots with your go-to club to three different targets. Full pre-shot routine every time. Score: inside 15 yards.
  • Wedges (30 min): 5 shots each to 50, 75 and 100 yards, twice through. Score: within 10 yards of the number.

On the course

  • Tee off with the club that keeps you in play, even if it's a hybrid.
  • Aim for the middle of every green. Flags are for later.
  • From trouble, get back to the fairway first. One shot back in play beats a hero shot.
  • Lag first, hole second. The goal from 30 feet is a tap-in.

Track these three numbers

Each round, write down penalty strokes, three-putts and double bogeys or worse. When those three numbers come down, 89 shows up on the card. Once it does, move to the break 80 practice plan.

Common questions

What do I need to do to break 90?

Avoid double bogeys more than you chase birdies. Keep the tee shot in play, have one approach shot you trust, and get down in three from just off the green.

How long does it take to break 90?

It depends on where you start, but a scored practice plan for four weeks will show you exactly which part of your game is holding you back.
