How to Introduce a Kid to Golf (4 Ways to Make It Fun)

Under Par Coaching Team · Updated November 27, 2026 · 2 min read

Golf is one of the few sports a parent and child can play together for decades. The trick is making the first experiences fun, not technical. Here are four ways to start.

1. Topgolf or the driving range

A plain range session loses most kids after about 20 balls. If you have a Topgolf nearby, start there: targets, games, scoring and food keep everyone engaged. Hit from an upper deck if there is one, and play the games rather than just hitting balls.

2. Mini golf

Nearly every kid loves mini golf. It shows them golf can be fun, teaches the putting stroke without anyone calling it a lesson, and introduces keeping score. Friendly competition and a little trash talk are encouraged.

3. Junior golf clinics

Clinics are relaxed and social, and kids learn faster with friends their own age. Check the clinic is set up for your child's age before signing up. Your local course or the PGA's junior programs are a good place to look.

4. Caddying

For older kids, caddying is a great way into the game. They earn money and build work habits, communication skills and confidence, while watching good golfers up close. Many courses need caddies. Ask the pro shop or caddie master whether they train young caddies.

Keep it fun

Short sessions, lots of variety, no swing lectures. When your kid starts asking to go, you'll know it worked. When they're ready for structure, our 30-minute practice plan is a good first step.
